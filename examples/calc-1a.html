The big picture

Why are we learning calculus?

Every topic on this map is one answer to a single question: how do things change? Back in algebra you could find the average rate of change between two points — the slope of a line. But the world rarely changes at a steady rate, and the questions that matter are about a single instant: how fast right now, the exact peak, the precise area.

The whole course is the machinery for answering that. Limits make “zooming in” precise, so we can shrink the gap between two points to nothing. That gives us the derivative — the instantaneous rate of change. The rules (power, product, chain, implicit) are shortcuts so you never have to compute that limit by hand. The applications (related rates, optimization, curve sketching) are what you actually do with a derivative once you have it.

So the map isn’t a list of unrelated tricks — it’s one idea unfolding. Follow the central arrow and every branch hangs off “rate of change.” Get comfortable with that thread and the rest of the course has something solid to stand on.
